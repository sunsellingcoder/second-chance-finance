BetterMinds Product Requirements Document - Rohan V
 
Second-Chance Financial Rebuilding Assistant

---

1. Overview

Problem: People leaving incarceration face a uniquely hard financial re-entry: damaged or absent credit history, multi-year gaps in financial activity, no guidance on where to start, and a banking system not built for their situation. Existing credit/finance apps (Credit Karma, NerdWallet, etc.) assume a continuous financial history and don't address re-entry-specific obstacles (ID recovery, banking access, employment gaps, restitution debt, predatory lending targeting this population).

Solution: 
1. Models a realistic, personalized credit-rebuilding timeline
2. Explains financial concepts in plain, simple language
3. Connects users to vetted "second-chance" banking and credit products

---

2. Target Users

User Type and Description
 
Primary: Individuals recently released from incarceration (0–5 years post-release)

Secondary: Reentry case managers, parole/probation officers, nonprofit reentry programs 

Tertiary: Family members helping a returning citizen rebuild finances 

Key constraints to design for:
- Limited or no smartphone/data plan access (some users on shared devices, library computers)

- Low digital literacy in some cases, high in others (do not assume)

- Possible lack of permanent address, phone number, or state ID

- Distrust of institution: need for privacy and non-judgmental tone

- Wide range of financial literacy baselines

 3. Goals & Success statistics

Primary goals:
- Help users understand *where they stand* and *what steps come next*
- Reduce time-to-first-financial-milestone (first bank account, first secured card, first credit score)
- Reduce predatory product usage (payday loans, high-fee prepaid cards)

Success metrics (MVP → V1):
- % of users who open a bank account within 30/60/90 days of using the tool
- % of users who establish a credit score within 6 months
- User-reported clarity/confidence score (simple in-app survey)
- Referral conversion rate to partner second-chance banking products
- Retention: % returning to check progress monthly

4. Core Features

Credit-Rebuilding Timeline Assistant

- Intake questionnaire: current financial status (bank account? ID? SSN card? existing debts? restitution? employment status?)

- Generates a personalized, realistic timeline ("Month 1: open a second-chance checking account → Month 2–3: apply for a secured credit card → Month 6: check credit report for errors")

- Model should be rules-based/deterministic at the core (credit scoring mechanics are well-documented — FICO/VantageScore factors), with AI used to narrate and adapt the plan

- Progress tracking: checklist format, milestone celebrations

- Adjusts dynamically as user completes steps or reports setbacks (e.g., denied application → offers alternate path)

4.2 Plain-Language Financial Explainer (AI Chat / Q&A)

- Conversational interface answering questions like "What is a secured credit card?" or "Why did my score drop?"

- Explicitly tuned for: short sentences, reading-level awareness (aim ~6th–8th grade reading level by default, adjustable)

- Should clarify it is educational, not licensed financial/legal advice

- Pre-built explainer library for common reentry-specific topics:

  - Restitution/court debt vs. credit debt (these are often confused)

  - How to get a copy of your credit report for free

  - Disputing errors on a credit report

  - Understanding predatory products to avoid (rent-to-own, payday loans, high-fee prepaid cards)

  - ID recovery basics (state-by-state, since this often blocks account opening)

4.3 Second-Chance Banking & Product Directory

- Curated, vetted database of:
  - Second-chance checking accounts (e.g., institutions using tools like ChexSystems alternatives)

  - Secured credit cards designed for credit builders

  - Credit-builder loans (community development financial institutions / CDFIs)
  - Nonprofit financial counseling orgs (especially reentry-specific ones)

- Filter by: state, whether a permanent address/ID is required, fees, credit-check requirements

- Clear labeling of any affiliate/referral relationships (trust and transparency are critical for this audience)

- "Red flag" warnings on predatory products the tool explicitly does NOT recommend

4.4 Case Manager / Organization Dashboard (V2+)

- Reentry organizations or parole officers could get a dashboard to track (with consent) client progress across a caseload

- Bulk resource sharing, printable PDF guides for clients without reliable device access

4.5 Offline/Low-Access Design

- Printable PDF version of the personalized plan (for library/kiosk use, no login needed to view a static plan)
- SMS-based check-ins/reminders as an alternative to app-only access
- Low-bandwidth mode/works on older Android devices

---

5. Example User Flow

1. User signs up (minimal: email or phone only, no address required)

2. Short intake: "Do you have a bank account? A state ID? Any existing debts?"

3. Tool generates a “first-30-days plan”: get a copy of your free credit report, open a second-chance checking account (3 nearby/online options shown)

4. User chats with the AI assistant: "What's a secured credit card?" - plain-language answer

5. User marks a step complete - timeline adjusts, next milestone unlocked

6. Monthly check-in nudges (email/SMS): "It's been 60 days,  want to check if you're ready for a secured card?"

6. How to Build It (Development Approach)

Phase 0 — Research & Validation
- Interview reentry nonprofits, formerly incarcerated individuals, and credit counselors
- Compile authoritative data sources:
  - CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau) guidance on credit building
  - FICO/VantageScore public scoring factor documentation
  - State-by-state ID recovery requirements
  - Directory of existing second-chance banking programs (ex: the Bank On coalition list is a good public starting point)

Phase 1 — MVP
stack suggestion:
- Frontend: simple responsive web app (React), mobile-first, works on low-end devices
- Backend: lightweight (Node/Python + Postgres) to store user intake + progress state
- AI layer: Claude/GPT API for the plain-language chat + plan narration, with **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** over your vetted resource library so answers stay grounded in real, current info rather than hallucinated
- Timeline logic: build as a deterministic rules engine (if/then decision tree) — do NOT let the LLM freely generate credit-scoring math or timelines; the LLM narrates output from your rules engine
- Resource directory: structured database (not scraped/generated) — this must be manually vetted for trustworthiness

MVP scope:
- Intake questionnaire
- Static-but-personalized timeline generator
- Basic AI chat Q&A (RAG-grounded)
- Directory of 10–20 vetted second-chance banking products/orgs (start with 1–2 states, then expand)

Phase 2 — V1 Expansion
- Progress tracking + milestone notifications
- SMS reminder system
- Expand the system to all 50 states
- Add downloadable/printable plan PDFs

Phase 3 — V2
- Case manager dashboard for nonprofits/parole orgs
- Partnerships with CDFIs and second-chance banks (potential referral revenue)
- Credit report import (via Plaid or similar) once users have enough history to check

7. Risks & Compliance Considerations

This is high-sensitivity. Build compliance.

- Not a licensed advisor: Clearly disclaim that the tool provides educational information, not financial or legal advice. Consider consulting a compliance lawyer before launch, given credit-related guidance.
- Fair lending/discrimination law: Any product recommendations must avoid anything resembling discriminatory steering.
- Data privacy
Incarceration history is deeply sensitive data. Minimize data collection, avoid storing criminal record details unless essential, use strong encryption, and be transparent about data use. Consider not requiring a real name/SSN at all in MVP.

- Avoid AI hallucination on financial facts: Use RAG grounded in vetted sources for anything factual (fees, eligibility, credit mechanics) — never let the LLM freely generate numbers or eligibility rules.

- Vetting partners carefully:
 Predatory lenders specifically target this population; any "second-chance" product must be rigorously vetted, and affiliate relationships must be disclosed.

- Accessibility: Design for varying literacy levels and limited device/data access — this audience cannot be assumed to have an iPhone and unlimited data.
